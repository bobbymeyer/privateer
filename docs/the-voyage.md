# the voyage

A design for the rebuild. Privateer becomes one game: the voyage of a
captain who has just watched Drake come home.

## the premise

Plymouth, autumn 1580. The *Golden Hind* is in, her hold full of Spanish
silver, and the Queen has knighted her captain on his own deck. Every
gentleman with a ship and a few friends at court thinks the same thing:
*I could do that too.*

You are one of them. Fit out at Plymouth on what you can raise, sail round
the world the way Drake did, or a way of your own, take what Spain leaves
lying about, and come home rich, or not at all. Most who tried came home
with less than they left with, or didn't come home. Fenton turned back off
Brazil in 1582. Cavendish made it round in 1586–88, and died at sea on his
second try.

## the shape of the journey

More linear than what we have, less than the trail: **a chain of landmarks
with forks**, on the real chart. Each leg sails itself along the sea lanes,
a day at a time, with the day's events. Between landmarks you can turn
aside to a port or a prize in reach, at the cost of the days. At a fork you
choose the route.

Drake's way, westward, is the main line:

1. **Plymouth.** Fit out.
2. **The Canaries or Cape Verde.** Last water before the crossing; Portuguese
   (and so, since 1580, Spanish) islands. Take a Portuguese prize for her
   pilot, as Drake did.
3. **The coast of Brazil and the River Plate.** Water, wood, seals and
   penguins for the pot.
4. **Port San Julián.** Winter over, careen, and settle the crew, Drake's way
   with Doughty or another.
5. **The fork at the bottom of the world:**
   - **the Strait of Magellan**: sheltered, slow, narrow, williwaws;
   - **round the Horn**: Drake's discovery after the storm, open sea, faster
     if you live.
6. **The coast of Chile and Peru.** Spain's soft underbelly: undefended
   towns and treasure ships that have never seen an enemy. The richest leg,
   and the one that raises the alarm.
7. **The treasure ship.** The *Cacafuego*'s kind, at sea off Panama: the set
   piece.
8. **The fork home:**
   - **across the Pacific** by Nova Albion or straight from New Spain, to the
     Ladrones, the Moluccas (cloves, and a sultan who hates the Portuguese),
     Java, the Cape of Good Hope, Sierra Leone, home: round the world;
   - **back the way you came**, through the Strait or round the Horn again,
     with the Spanish waiting: shorter, and they know you're coming.
9. **Plymouth.** The score.

Turning back is always possible, Fenton's way. You keep what you have.

## what wears down

Every day at sea costs:

- **provisions**: a ration a hand a day; short rations stretch them and
  hurt health and temper;
- **water**: casks, which go faster in the tropics;
- **health**: scurvy creeps in after about six weeks without fresh food and
  kills slowly, then quickly; the flux and fever come in hot ports;
- **the hull**: weed, worm and weather; careening at a landmark mends it;
- **canvas and timber**: storms take them, and without spares a storm takes
  the hull instead;
- **temper**: the crew's patience, as morale now: hunger, sickness, a long
  leg and no prize sour it, and at the bottom it's mutiny.

The crew is a number (say 80 at the start) plus the hands of note, named.
Fewer hands, the ship sails worse and fights worse (fewer cards). Too few
and she can't be sailed at all.

## what you do at a landmark

Water. Forage or fish (a roll, better with fresh crews). Trade, where they'll
deal with you. Careen. Rest (health back, days gone). Raid, where it's
Spanish and worth it. Choose the next leg.

## the fight

The card fight stays, as the trail's hunting: the action you choose to
enter. Prizes, raids, and the ships that come at you. What changes is that
it feeds the survival: powder and shot are spent, hands are lost (hits on
you kill crew as well as hull), and a prize's stores (water, biscuit, wine,
canvas) are worth as much as her silver.

## the 1580s

Strictly. Spain and Portugal are one crown from 1580, so every Iberian port
from Lisbon to Goa to Manila is hostile. No Dutch East India Company, no
Batavia, no English colonies, no Port Royal, no Quebec. France is divided
and its Huguenots friendly; the Barbary corsairs hostile to everyone. In
the Moluccas the Sultan of Ternate is at war with the Portuguese and glad of
an enemy of theirs.

## what goes

The sandbox systems that don't serve the voyage: letters of marque, the
drifting war table, renown, the standing table by nation (folded into one
thing, Spain's alarm), wages (folded into rations), Barbary raiding, the
guard of raided towns (kept only if it fits the alarm).

What stays: the chart and its lanes, the living sea, the card fight and its
chain, prize cards, events that ask you, the logbook, today's voyage.

## the score

Treasure home, plus every hand alive, plus the ship's condition, against
the voyages of the time:

| a voyage like | means |
| --- | --- |
| Drake's | round the world, rich, most of the crew home |
| Cavendish's | round the world, rich, half the crew lost |
| Fenton's | home again, poorer, having turned back |
| Oxenham's | lost |

## the phases

1. **The 1580s world**: ports and nations of the time; Spain and Portugal
   one crown.
2. **The journey**: landmarks, forks, legs sailed along the lanes, detours.
3. **Stores and survival**: provisions, water, health, canvas, crew;
   pace and rations; the outfitting at Plymouth.
4. **The events**: the trail's random events, as choices where it matters.
5. **The fight feeds survival**: powder, crew lost, stores taken.
6. **Cut the sandbox**: letters, wars, renown and the rest.
7. **The score**, the end screen, the logbook.
